# Banned Phrases

Grouped by category; each heading carries the fix.

## Throat-clearing openers — delete, then state the point

"Here's the thing", "Here's what/why/how ...", "The truth is", "Let me be clear", "It turns out", "The uncomfortable truth is", "I'll be honest", "Let me explain", "Can we talk about"

## Filler kill-list — delete, or use the short form

"It's important to note that", "It's worth noting", "In order to" (→ "to"), "Simply", "Just", "Basically", "Essentially", "Actually", "As mentioned earlier", "As we discussed", "As you can see", "At its core", "At the end of the day", "When it comes to", "Additionally" / "Furthermore" (usually deletable)

## Emphasis crutches — delete; content carries its own weight

"Full stop." / "Period.", "Let that sink in", "Make no mistake", "This matters because", "Here's why that matters"

## Business jargon — swap for the plain word

navigate → handle, unpack → explain, lean into → accept, landscape → situation, deep dive → analysis, double down → commit, game-changer → name the actual change, moving forward → next, circle back → revisit, on the same page → agreed

## Vague declaratives — replace with the specific thing

"The implications are significant", "The reasons are structural", "The stakes are high", "This is the deepest problem", "The consequences are real". Name the implication, the reason, the stake.

## Meta-commentary — delete; the text moves, it never narrates itself

"Let me walk you through", "In this section we'll", "As we'll see", "I want to explore", "Plot twist:", "Spoiler:", "Hint:", "But that's another post", "X is a feature, not a bug"

## Hedging — state it or omit it

"I think" / "I believe" (before a checkable fact), "sort of", "kind of", "arguably", "It could be said", "perhaps" as a reflex, "Not always. Not perfectly."

## Praise filler — delete and answer

"Great question!", "Excellent point!", "I'll help you with that", "Happy to help", "I'd be glad to"

## Adverbs — cut the decorative, keep the precise

Decorative ones die: "really", "truly", "genuinely", "honestly", "literally", "deeply", "fundamentally". Precise ones live: "atomically", "lazily", "idempotently", "recursively".
